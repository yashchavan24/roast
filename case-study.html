Project 3 · Brief-to-Output Case Study

From one-line brief to a full campaign system.

"We're a small-batch D2C coffee brand. Launch our limited-edition dark roast to people who buy coffee at midnight. Playful, but premium. Make it feel scarce." Fictional client brief · Roast Theory® · deliverables: social + display

The insight I chose to build on

Midnight coffee buyers aren't buying caffeine — they're buying permission to stay up. So instead of advertising the coffee, the campaign advertises the moment: 1 a.m., quiet, yours. That gave me the creative platform — "For the 1 a.m. crowd" — and a visual metaphor the whole system hangs on: a crema-colored full moon that reads as moon, coffee lid, and crema in one shape.

Two directions I killed (and why)

Direction A · Rejected

"Bean There" — bright & illustrated

Pastel palette, wavy retro type, smiling bean mascot. The obvious "fun coffee brand" move.

Why it diedLooked like every D2C coffee brand on Instagram. Zero scarcity — scarcity needs night, and this palette was a sunny café. Premium and playful don't survive pastels.
Direction B · Rejected

"Roast Index" — technical & swiss

Monospace data-viz, tasting-note charts, grid everything. Very clever, very cold.

Why it diedAppealed to coffee nerds but excluded the casual 1 a.m. buyer. Emotion was missing — and emotional recall is what makes an ad work at midnight when nobody's comparison-shopping.

The winning direction: "Midnight Roast"

Dark, warm, and scarce — the night sky does the selling.

Near-black grounds the premium feel; crema-tan and burnt orange keep it warm, not gloomy. One repeated shape — the crema moon — makes 7 formats read as one brand. Scarcity is built into the copy everywhere: 500 bags, then it's gone.

Type & motif

Fraunces — for the voice. Italic serif for the punchline.

Inter — for everything the voice needs to be legible: eyebrows, offers, CTAs. One shape rules the system: the crema moon.

Final outputs — one campaign, four formats

Every piece keeps the same headline language, the moon, and the offer. Format changes the layout, never the brand.

What I'd carry into the next brief

  • One metaphor beats ten ideas. The crema moon made every resize decision obvious — if it's not the moon's moment, the format doesn't need it.
  • Scarcity is a design constraint. "500 bags" shaped the palette (night), the type (urgent italics), and the CTA (Shop the drop) — not just the copy.
  • Design for the smallest unit first. The 160×600 forced the message down to five words; if it works there, it works everywhere.
